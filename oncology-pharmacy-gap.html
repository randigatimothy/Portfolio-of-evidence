The gap between the guideline and the ward

21 Apr 2026

Most adherence figures quoted for oral anticancer therapies come from trial populations — closely monitored, well-resourced, following a strict protocol. Almost none of that resembles the patients I dispensed to. This post is a working note on why that gap matters, and why I think it's a legitimate research question rather than just a practice grievance.

What the guideline assumes

Clinical guidelines for oral anticancer regimens generally assume a patient who can attend follow-up, afford the medicine consistently, and understand and manage side effects without much support. Trial-derived adherence figures are built on that assumption too, because trial participants are, by design, more able to meet it.

What the registry can't see

Population-level cancer registries record diagnoses, treatments, and outcomes — but not, in most cases, whether a patient actually took their medicine as prescribed in between. That's the layer my pharmacy background sits in, and it's largely invisible to the datasets epidemiologists usually work with.

My developing research question tries to sit in that gap: using dispensing records as a proxy for real-world adherence, and testing whether that proxy relates to outcomes captured at a population level. It's still a proposal, not a finding — but naming the gap clearly feels like the first real piece of research work I've done.
