A pharmacist's first month inside the epidemiology literature

4 Mar 2026

Pharmacy training gives you a very particular relationship with evidence: you learn to trust a randomised controlled trial, apply a dosing guideline, and move on. Epidemiology reads completely differently. A month into the literature for this MPhil, the thing that's taken longest to sit right is how comfortable epidemiologists are with uncertainty that never fully resolves — confidence intervals instead of cut-offs, hazard ratios instead of "does it work".

The vocabulary shift

Confounding was the first real stumbling block. In practice, if a patient on a particular regimen did badly, my instinct was to look at the regimen. Epidemiology insists I also ask what else is different about that patient — age, comorbidity, socioeconomic access to follow-up care — before I can say anything about the regimen itself. It's a more honest way of thinking, but it's slower, and slower doesn't come naturally after years of needing an answer before the next patient reaches the counter.

Where my background actually helps

Reading adherence studies, I keep noticing what the data can't see: the patient who split tablets because of cost, the one who stopped for a side effect they never reported. Epidemiological method gives me rigour I didn't have. Practice gives me a sense of where the data is probably lying to itself. Neither is enough alone.
