Cancer Epidemiology

Incidence, prevalence, and the numbers behind cancer burden

12 Mar 2026

This session worked through South African cancer registry data directly, which was more useful than any definition slide could have been. Incidence and prevalence sound interchangeable in casual conversation; they are not, and mixing them up changes the story a set of numbers tells.

What the registry actually captures

Registry data is built from diagnoses, which means it reflects detection as much as it reflects true disease burden. A rising incidence figure can mean more cancer, or it can mean better screening and reporting finding cancer that was always there. Reading burden data now means asking which of those is doing the work before drawing any conclusion.

Where this connects to my research direction

Registries are the population-level half of the question I'm trying to build a thesis around. They can show outcomes at scale, but — as I noted in a recent blog post — they say very little about the treatment-taking behaviour sitting underneath those outcomes. This module gave me the vocabulary to describe that limitation properly instead of just gesturing at it.
