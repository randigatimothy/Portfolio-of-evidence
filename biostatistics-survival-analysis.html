First pass at survival analysis

19 Feb 2026

Survival analysis is the first statistics I've met that's genuinely built for the kind of data oncology produces: not everyone reaches the event (relapse, death, discontinuation) within the study window, and pretending they did — or just excluding them — throws away real information.

Censoring, finally clicking

I'd read the word "censoring" a dozen times before this week without it landing. What made it click: a patient who's alive and event-free at the end of the study isn't a "no" — they're an unfinished observation. Kaplan-Meier curves are built specifically to use that partial information instead of discarding it.

Why an average is the wrong question

My instinct from practice was to ask "what's the average time to X" — which is exactly the wrong question for time-to-event data with censoring involved. The right question is closer to "what proportion of patients are event-free by time T", which is a different shape of thinking entirely, and one I'm still building intuition for.
