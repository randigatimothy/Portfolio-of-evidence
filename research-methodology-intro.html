Research Methodology

Framing a research question in oncology

5 Feb 2026

First real module of the MPhil, and the first lesson was uncomfortable: most of the clinical questions I've carried out of practice are not yet research questions. They're concerns, or hunches, or patterns I've noticed — useful raw material, but not framed in a way anyone could actually go and test.

PICO, applied to my own questions

We worked through the PICO framework (Population, Intervention/Exposure, Comparison, Outcome) against questions we each brought from our own backgrounds. Mine — "why do some patients not take oral anticancer therapy as prescribed" — fell apart almost immediately under PICO. No defined population, no comparison group, no measurable outcome. Useful humility early on.

Feasibility as a first filter

The module's other main point: a research question has to be feasible before it's interesting. Access to data, ethical approval, time within the MPhil's timeline — all of that narrows the question before the "interesting" part even gets discussed. I left this session with a rougher, smaller version of my original question, which felt like a loss at the time and now looks more like progress.
