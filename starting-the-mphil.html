Why I left the dispensary for the lab bench

10 Feb 2026

For six years, my understanding of cancer treatment came almost entirely from a dispensary counter. I saw prescriptions arrive, I counselled patients on how to take them, and I saw some of those patients come back — sometimes to collect a refill on schedule, sometimes weeks late, sometimes not at all. What I never had access to was the layer underneath: why the gaps happened, and what they meant for outcomes at any scale bigger than the person in front of me.

That's the honest answer to why I applied for an MPhil in Cancer Science rather than staying in practice. Not because I'd lost interest in patients, but because I kept running into questions practice couldn't answer. Why did adherence to oral anticancer therapies vary so much between patients on what looked like identical regimens? Why did the guideline literature rarely match what I was seeing walk through the door? I didn't have a research vocabulary for those questions yet — I had clinical instinct, which is a different thing.

What I'm hoping this MPhil gives me

Three things, roughly. A methodological toolkit — biostatistics and epidemiology, specifically, since that's where my instinct has always outrun my training. A research question I can actually defend, rather than a vague sense that "something's off" between guideline and practice. And, honestly, the discipline of writing this all down as I go, which is what this site is for.

The dispensary taught me what questions to ask. This degree is teaching me how to ask them properly.

I expect the posts that follow to be less tidy than this one — more working-through than reflection. That's intentional. A portfolio of evidence should show the thinking, not just the conclusions.
